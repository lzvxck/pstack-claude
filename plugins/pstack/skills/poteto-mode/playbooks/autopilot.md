### Autopilot

**You own the verdicts, never the landing. One owner runs each PR from build to verified merge-ready, and the operator lands everything herself.** For "autopilot this queue", "full autopilot", "stack them, don't ship", and one-owner-per-PR programs. The job is a queue of PRs handed over to drive to verified merge-ready with full autonomy. Orchestrate runs a standing program with a coordinator and a store; here each PR's owner carries the whole lifecycle to merge-ready, and the root keeps only verification, countersigns, and audits. Nothing in this playbook merges, arms auto-merge, or closes a PR; merging is always the operator's click.

1. **Mark the operator's items and honor state-then-wait.** Items the operator names for her own review stay hers. When she asks for the protocol or the plan to be stated, deliver the statement and stop. Execution starts only on her explicit go. On that go, write the full program objective to a standing-orders file (the program's `.audit/<slug>-goal.md`, or the orchestrate store when one exists) and re-read it at every audit tick. The objective continues across turns until the queue is done.
2. **Spawn one owner per PR with the full lifecycle.** One background agent per PR, in its own worktree, owns build, the PR per `playbooks/opening-a-pr.md` (which posts the `@claude review` trigger), self-proof on the real artifact (the **prove-it-works** principle skill), skeptical review-bot triage per `../references/review-bot-triage.md`, a slop-strip (the `deslop` skill from the `cursor-team-kit` plugin (`/cursor-team-kit:deslop`)), `/pstack:no-comments` (the **no-comments** skill), a rebase onto current trunk, and the babysit loop to green (`playbooks/babysit.md`). The rebase always precedes babysit and never waits for drift or conflicts. Every owner keeps a decisions.tsv trail per the **show-me-your-work** skill, never committed, returned with its reports. The owner's terminal state is merge-ready; step 4 gates whether it may be reported as such.
3. **Run owners in true parallel when the work is self-contained; chain when it is sequenced.** Many owners at once when PRs are self-contained: one writer per branch, disjoint files, cross-PR drift absorbed by rebase, each PR branched straight off main. Sequenced or coupled work becomes one linear chain of PRs, each based on its parent branch. An owner pushes only its own branch, `git push --force-with-lease` after an ls-remote check, and reports its tip and intended parent. The root owns chain topology: base retargets (`gh pr edit <n> --base <parent>`) go through the root only, because retargeting from inside a lane severs descendants.
4. **Swarm-verify every merge-ready head before it is reported.** At the owner's merge-ready head SHA, fan out parallel independent verifiers per the **swarm** skill and aggregate to one verdict. The fan-out mechanics live there; do not restate them. The lanes: re-run the gates at that SHA; prove the load-bearing behavior live on the real surface the change touches (`control-cli` or `control-ui` from `cursor-team-kit` as the change demands); audit the receipts and the diff, distrusting the PR body. The live lane is the floor, and a verdict without it is not clean. Nothing is reported merge-ready without the root's clean verdict, posted on the PR so the record outlives the chat. Findings go back to the owner for fix-forward, and the new head gets a fresh swarm and a fresh verdict.
5. **On a clean verdict the PR joins the deliverable and the owner takes the next item.** The verdict is made at a trunk-current head and pins that SHA. If trunk moves again before the operator lands it, the patch-id rule in `playbooks/shipping.md` governs re-verification; a new head voids the verdict unless the patch-id is unchanged. A rebase of the chain rewrites every SHA above it, so compare `git patch-id` at each verdict SHA against the new head and send anything that drifted back through step 4 before delivery.
6. **Run the root layer.** A genuinely new raise of a pinned gate or budget value (a limit CI only lets tighten) needs your fresh countersign, granted only after verifier proof. Absorbing values that already landed on main is drift, not a raise. Run an audit tick over all owners roughly every 30 minutes, armed as a real wake: the `/loop` skill in dynamic mode, or a background-shell 30-minute sleep whose completion is the wake. Never leave the cadence to memory or lossy completion notifications. At each tick, re-read this playbook from the installed plugin (`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/poteto-mode/playbooks/autopilot.md`), then re-read the standing-orders file. Audit the operation against both. Fix drift during that tick and treat it as urgent. Probe each owner by side effects only: commits, pushes, PR or check deltas, and store reports. Treat a lane that passes its expected runtime without a side effect as stuck. Stand it down and dispatch a replacement at once. Do not wait for a polite return. Collect the decision trails at each tick.
7. **Deliver merge-ready, then stop.** The deliverable is the queue of verified PRs: independent PRs each reported merge-ready with its verdict, or one linear chain reviewable bottom-up on GitHub, every link carrying its verifier verdict in a PR comment, with the bottom-up merge order stated for the operator. She reviews and lands with her own clicks. Watching her drain, if she asks for it, follows Shipping step 6.
8. **Stand down instantly on the operator's stop.** Her hold or stand-down reaches every owner as a zero-writes order immediately. Owners hold their briefs until she releases them.

**Reply:** the queue with each PR's owner, state, and head SHA; each verdict and the swarm that produced it; the merge order for the operator; countersigns granted and why; open operator gates; where the collected decision trails live.
